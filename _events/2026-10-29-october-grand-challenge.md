Multimodality is a virtually ubiquitous feature of communication. With the
increasing interest in how animals, including humans, use multimodal and
multicomponent signals in social interactions, there is an acute need for
standardised and rigorous tools that will allow us to visualise and analyse
these signals as they occur in naturalistic interactions — as a complex,
integrated system.

This Grand Challenge presents two frameworks that use **network theory**, a
powerful methodology for intuitively visualising and investigating the
relationships between entities, as a tool for analysing multimodal repertoires.

Hosted by **Séverine Hex**.

## Talk 1 — A network-based framework for multimodal repertoires: structure, meaning, and function (Séverine Hex)

Séverine Hex presents a framework that leverages network-based methods to
(1) describe the relationships between simultaneously produced signals within
and between modalities and (2) infer signal meaning and function. She embeds
these tools in a theoretical framework that can be used to interpret and
describe both the global structure of the repertoire and the role individual
signals play in shaping and modulating meaning.

## Talk 2 — Characterising communicative complexity across taxa with cross-modal repertoire networks (Louise Peckre)

Louise Peckre presents a framework based on network analyses of cross-modal
signal repertoires that aims to characterise communicative systems across taxa
using easily quantifiable data. This framework addresses complexity at the
level of the whole communicative system and allows communicative complexity to
be quantified in a multidimensional way, potentially helping to disentangle the
evolutionary trade-offs underlying the evolution of communicative complexity.

## Speakers

**Séverine Hex** (Harvard University; Junior Fellow, Harvard Society of Fellows)
is a comparative ethologist and behavioural ecologist who studies how animals
living in complex societies navigate the challenges of their social worlds using
multimodal communication, social cognition, and social bonds.

**Louise Peckre** (University Sorbonne Paris Nord; Associate Professor) is a
behavioural and evolutionary biologist. Her research examines the evolution of
social behaviour and communication across taxa, focusing on how social and
ecological factors shape signalling strategies. She is particularly interested
in multimodal communication, the interplay between communicative and social
complexity, and the cognitive and functional underpinnings of behavioural
diversity.
